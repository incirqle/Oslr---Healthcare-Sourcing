Outreach to registered nurses

A schedule-first four-email sequence for staff and specialty RNs.

On this page

  1. Cadence
  2. Email 1: Introduction (Day 0)
  3. Email 2: Follow-up (Day 3)
  4. Email 3: The numbers (Day 8)
  5. Email 4: Close (Day 14)
  6. Notes by unit
  7. Before you send

A four-email sequence over 14 days for staff and specialty RNs in ICU, OR, ER, L&D and oncology. Practical and schedule-first, because nurses compare offers on shift, ratios and culture as much as pay.

Best for: hospital-employed staff roles, specialty units, travel-to-staff conversion. Cadence: 4 emails over 14 days. RNs usually respond on a shorter cycle than physicians. Typical reply rate: 12 to 22 percent, depending on unit, location and shift.

Cadence

Email Day Purpose
1 Day 0 Introduction: role, unit, shift, location
2 Day 3 One specific detail about the unit or schedule
3 Day 8 Concrete numbers: ratios, differentials, ramp
4 Day 14 Polite close

Stop on any reply.

Email 1: Introduction (Day 0)

Subject: [first_name], [unit_type] RN role at [hospital_name] in [role_city]

Hi [first_name], I'm [your_first_name] with [hospital_name] in [role_city]. I came across your profile and wanted to reach out about a [unit_type] RN position. The role is [shift_pattern: 3x12s days, rotating nights, weekend program]. The unit is [unit_specifics: bed count, patient population, trauma level]. We are hiring for [start_window]. If you would like to learn more, even just to compare with where you are now, I would value a call. Thanks, [your_first_name] [your_title], [hospital_name] [your_phone]

Email 2: Follow-up (Day 3)

Subject: Re: [first_name], [unit_type] role at [hospital_name]

Hi [first_name], Following up on my note from a few days ago. One thing I should have mentioned: [one_specific_detail: typical ratio, charge nurse coverage, in-house intensivist, self-scheduling, education benefits, certification support]. It tends to matter most to nurses looking at [unit_type] roles. If you are open to it, here is my calendar: [calendar_link]. Thanks, [your_first_name]

Email 3: The numbers (Day 8)

Subject: [first_name], what the role looks like

Hi [first_name], I won't keep writing if this is not right, but here are the numbers, since that is what matters when you compare offers: Patient ratio: [typical_ratio] Shift differential: [night, weekend, specialty] Base hourly: [range_for_experience] Sign-on bonus: [if offered, with commitment terms] PTO: [accrual] Education: [tuition, certification support, clinical ladder] If that is in range, here is my calendar: [calendar_link]. If not, I will send one more note and stop. [your_first_name]

Email 4: Close (Day 14)

Subject: Closing the loop, [first_name]

Hi [first_name], This is my last note. The last thing you need after a run of shifts is more email from me. If your timing changes, or a colleague comes to mind, just reply here. Wishing you well. [your_first_name] [your_title], [hospital_name]

Notes by unit

  • ICU, MICU, SICU: lead with intensivist coverage and ratio (1:1 or 1:2). Name the trauma level. Mention CCRN support if you offer it.
  • OR: name the case mix and number of ORs. First-call and second-call structure matters.
  • ER: trauma level comes first. Be direct about volume and acuity.
  • L&D: births per year is the headline number. Name your NICU level.
  • Oncology: name medical, surgical or radiation oncology, and infusion or inpatient.

Before you send

  • Lead with the shift. For most RNs, shift pattern is the first filter.
  • Give real ratios. "1:1 in MICU, 1:2 in step-down" is information. "Competitive ratios" is not.
  • Name the unit. A SICU nurse and a MICU nurse have different experience.
  • Promise only what you offer. Nurses check certification benefits against the offer letter.
