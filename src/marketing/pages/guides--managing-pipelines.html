Managing your pipeline

Using Projects, statuses, notes and Contacts to keep a large pipeline easy to work.

On this page

  1. One project per role
  2. Use status to keep things moving
  3. Write notes a colleague could pick up
  4. Review weekly, not daily
  5. Projects or Contacts
  6. Closing a project
  7. Patterns to avoid

Finding candidates is the first half. Keeping track of them is the second. Here is how to use Projects, statuses, notes and Contacts so a 200-person pipeline stays easy to work.

One project per role

Avoid a single project called Cardiology, open roles. Smaller projects are easier to act on and easier to close.

A good project name tells you the role and the place:

  • Better: Spine surgeon, Mayo Rochester, ICU RN, DFW, FM residents, 2027 grads
  • Weaker: Cardiology search v3, Q2 hires

When in doubt, start a new project instead of reusing an old one.

Use status to keep things moving

Every saved candidate has a status. A common flow:

Unreviewed → Reviewed → Reaching out → Replied → Interested → Interviewing → Hired, Passed or Not now

Rules that work for most recruiters:

  • Nothing stays Unreviewed for more than three days. Review it or close the project.
  • Reaching out with no reply for 14 days moves to Not now. Try again in 60 to 90 days.
  • Every Replied candidate gets a next step within a day.

If every status column is about the same size, candidates are not moving. A healthy pipeline narrows.

Write notes a colleague could pick up

Each candidate in a project has notes. Write them so someone else, or you in three months, can act on them cold.

  • Better: Spoke Apr 15. Spouse finishing radiology fellowship at Brigham. Family will not move before June 2027. Open to a July 2027 start.
  • Weaker: Talked, going well.

A good note says when, what, why it matters and what happens next.

Review weekly, not daily

A weekly review keeps most pipelines clean in under an hour:

  1. Open Analytics and find projects with stalled candidates.
  2. Open the project that needs the most attention and sort by time in current status.
  3. Move ten candidates forward, to Not now, or to Passed.
  4. Pick three candidates to follow up with next week.

Projects or Contacts

Projects show candidates by role. Contacts shows every clinician whose details you have revealed, across all projects.

Use Contacts to: - Check whether you have already worked with someone before reaching out - Look someone up when you forget which project they are in - Reuse contact details you have already revealed

Use Projects to: - Work one role and see only the people you are considering for it - Move candidates through your hiring steps - Export a shortlist to CSV for your ATS or hiring manager

Closing a project

Close a project when the role is filled, when it is no longer open, or when you have stopped working it. Closed projects keep their history, and every candidate stays in Contacts.

Once a quarter, close anything you have not touched in 60 days. The list gets shorter and what remains is the work that matters.

Patterns to avoid

  • The catch-all project. If a candidate is worth saving, they belong to a specific role.
  • Silent status changes. A move to Not now with no note becomes a mystery later.
  • Duplicate projects. Cardiology, Houston and Houston cardiologist search hold the same people. Keep one.
