Sourcing healthcare talent

Search patterns and market notes for physicians, residents, APPs, nurses, CRNAs and therapists.

On this page

  4. Registered nurses
  5. CRNAs

  7. Principles that hold across roles

Practical patterns for sourcing physicians, advanced practice providers, nurses and therapists across specialties and markets.

Physicians in practice

The market

Fellowship-trained physicians are a small, competitive pool. Most are passive: employed, comfortable and not looking. Cold outreach to attendings gets low response; warm introductions do far better.

Searches that work

  • [Specialty] in [metro] for community and private-practice roles
  • [Subspecialty] at [health system] for academic and large-system roles
  • [Specialty] with 10+ years experience in [region] for senior roles

Keep in mind

  • Board certification is implied for an attending search. You do not need to add it.
  • Geographic ties beat salary. Most attendings will not move for pay alone. Lead with location and family ties.
  • Subspecialty is a strong filter. Structural heart cardiologists and interventional cardiologists are different audiences.

Residents and fellows

The market

The recruiting window keeps moving earlier. Competitive fellows in cardiology, GI, orthopedics and surgical subspecialties often commit 6 to 12 months before training ends. Family medicine residents usually decide in the fall of their final year.

Searches that work

  • PGY-5 [specialty] residents in [region]
  • Current [specialty] fellows at [program]
  • Final-year [specialty] residents at [health system]

Keep in mind

  • Oslr reads the training year from start dates. A PGY-5 search returns people in their fifth year now, not everyone with a resident title.
  • Hometown ties matter most. Many residents want to practice where they grew up or where a partner has roots, not where they train.
  • Earlier outreach builds the relationship. Later outreach converts better but faces more competition.
  • Use the right term. A cardiology fellow has already finished an internal medicine residency.

Nurse practitioners and physician assistants

The market

APP demand has outpaced supply for a decade. Strong APPs in primary care, urgent care and surgical specialties field several offers. Practice setting and scope of practice drive most decisions.

Searches that work

  • Nurse practitioners in [specialty] in [metro]
  • Physician assistants at [organization]
  • Family nurse practitioners with 5+ years experience in [region]

Keep in mind

  • State scope rules differ. An NP can practice independently in some states and not others. Say which model your role uses.
  • Specialty matters more than credential. A primary care NP and an urgent care NP want different jobs.
  • NP and PA are different credentials. Run separate searches if both fit the role.

Registered nurses

The market

The RN market is local and shift-driven. Specialty units (ICU, OR, ER, L&D, oncology) compete for a smaller credentialed pool. Travel nurses converting to staff roles are a real channel.

Searches that work

  • ICU nurses with CCRN in [metro]
  • Registered nurses at [hospital]
  • Nurses with [specialty] experience near [city]

Keep in mind

  • Credentials narrow fast. A CCRN-required search returns far fewer people than CCRN-preferred. Many experienced ICU nurses do not hold CCRN.
  • Adult, pediatric and neonatal are separate searches. Search PICU and NICU on their own.
  • Travel nurses are real candidates. One finishing a contract is often weighing staff offers.
  • Schedule and ratios lead. Open with the shift and the unit, not only total pay.

CRNAs

The market

CRNA supply is limited by the number of training programs, so most searches need to span several metros. Quality of life and call structure drive decisions.

Searches that work

  • CRNAs in [region]
  • Certified registered nurse anesthetists at [health system]
  • Student CRNAs in [state] for graduates making their first practice decision

Keep in mind

  • Established CRNAs rarely relocate. Lean on hometown and partner ties.
  • Setting decides the job. Hospital, ASC and CRNA-only practice are different roles. Say which one in your outreach.

Therapists and allied health

The market

Supply varies widely. Inpatient PT, OT and SLP roles at large systems are usually well supplied. Pediatric OT, NICU respiratory therapy and other specialty roles are tight.

Searches that work

  • [Role] in [metro]
  • [Role] with [specialty] experience
  • [Role] at [organization]

Keep in mind

  • Name the role. "Therapist" mixes PT, OT, SLP and RT. Be specific.
  • Setting matters as much as specialty. Inpatient, outpatient, home health and school-based are different jobs.

Principles that hold across roles

  1. Specific searches win. Narrow to the smallest pool you can credibly recruit from.
  2. Geography is harder than it looks. Search where candidates are and where they want to be.
  3. Keep the relationship. Use Oslr to find people and keep their context, then reach out like a person.
  4. The market moves. Consolidation, visa policy and telehealth keep changing who is open to a move. Re-run important searches each quarter.
