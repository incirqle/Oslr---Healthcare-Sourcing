# Analytics

Analytics shows you what's happening across your Oslr account — searches you've run, projects you're working, and (when Campaigns ships) outreach performance.

The Analytics section is most useful once you have several weeks of activity. Day one it'll be sparse.

---

## What's in Analytics

Analytics is organized around three views:

### Search activity
- Total searches run, broken down by week and month
- Most-used queries
- Average results per search
- How often you applied or removed filters mid-search

Useful for: spotting which kinds of searches you run most, and identifying queries that consistently return too few or too many results.

### Project activity
- Number of active projects
- Candidates added per project, per week
- Status distribution across projects (how many candidates in Reviewed, Reaching out, Interested, etc.)
- Time-to-first-status-change for new candidates

Useful for: spotting projects that are stalling or projects with bloated "Unreviewed" backlogs.

### Outreach performance *(when Campaigns ships)*
- Send volume, open rate, reply rate, unsubscribe rate
- Performance by campaign, by sequence step, and by job-type segment
- Time-to-reply distribution
- Best-performing subject lines

Useful for: tuning sequences and identifying what messaging works for which audience.

---

## How to use Analytics well

Once a week is the right cadence for most users. Spend 10 minutes scanning Analytics on a Friday afternoon and you'll catch:

- A project with too many candidates stuck in Unreviewed (you need to clear the backlog or close the project)
- A query you've run six times that always returns 0 results (something's wrong with the phrasing)
- A campaign with a high unsubscribe rate (something's off about the audience or the message)

Don't optimize on a daily basis. Daily noise is louder than weekly signal.
