- Institutional email models are evidence interpreters, not mailbox verifiers; deterministic checks must re-validate every sample, identity, domain, pattern and collision before delivery. Why: model confidence is not measured ownership or deliverability.
- Coworkers, not company websites, decide an employer's mail domain and pattern (`institution-election.ts`): an example counts only when the address spells that coworker's own name; the domain must carry the employer's distinctive name, acronym or listed domain; at least three people, 80% agreement, unanimity under six, and the target's role group must not contradict. Research checks six likely email holders before any broader search or batch, and unclear evidence escalates rather than weakening these rules. A listed website or link page is a hint, never a gate. Why: hospitals list link pages and careers sites, provider 'verified' flags mark deliverability, not ownership, and clean hospitals should not pay for the maximum research path.
- Contact classification uses a server-resolved employer domain; moving an already-owned institutional address from personal to work never incurs a second charge. Why: provider buckets are not authoritative and customers must not pay twice for the same email.
